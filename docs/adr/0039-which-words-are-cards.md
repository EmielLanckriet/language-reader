# ADR-0039: Which words are cards, and which new ones come first
Status: Accepted. Date: 2026-10-05. Amends: [ADR-0027](0027-encounter-log-and-derived-memory.md) (what makes a card) and spec 007 research R8 (new-card order). Issue #5.

A word is an **active** card, coming due on its own schedule, when it was reviewed in Reader, seen
in Anki (an Anki seed), or is a **known** word: never tapped, and met untapped in two separate
sessions answered "I tapped every word I didn't know", with the line's English not shown. A word
tapped at least once is a **candidate**. The daily new-word budget (More → Cards, "New
words per day", kept per device) picks candidates by their rank in **general Chinese frequency**,
most frequent first. Any other word is not a card. The library's "due come up" counts active cards
only.

Why: on 2026-10-05 the Cards screen showed 302 due, which made the queue unworkable. Measured on the
phone, they were all Anki-seeded words (2,121 seeded, 310 due by then); 458 tapped words had never
been reviewed and none had been reviewed in Reader. The reader decided that Reader replaces Anki,
so seeded words stay active as they are. They also reframed the question from "which words will I
remember" to "which words are worth investing in", by cards or by watching: a word's value is how
often it comes up in Chinese in general. How often it comes up in the reader's own library does not
matter, since those words are met by watching anyway, so library frequency no longer orders new
cards. Known words go in the deck outside the budget: their reading history gives them a memory,
so their reviews are light.

Literature behind the shape (not the numbers): repetition helps incidental learning with a medium
effect and no threshold (Uchihara, Webb & Yanagisawa 2019); about ten encounters give sizeable
gains (Webb 2007); words met fewer than eight times in a graded reader were not retained after
three months (Waring & Takaki 2003). Cards are the deliberate boost for words worth having that
input alone will not teach soon.

How:
- `memory.ts`: `WordEvidence.known` counts the distinct sessions whose untapped exposures already
  count as "seen" evidence (answer `all`, not helped, not tapped in that session); `known` needs
  `KNOWN_SESSIONS` (2) and no tap ever. The reading memory carries `known: true`; the derived
  `memory.card` column stores 2 for it (0 no card, 1 card). The rule key gains `+known-2`, so the
  sweep recomputes every memory once.
- `queue.ts`: `isActive` (reviewed, seeded or known) decides due cards; candidates are the cards
  that are not active, ordered by `rank`, a word's position in the frequency list (unlisted words
  last). `shares.ts` uses `isActive` for the library's due counts.
- `src/lib/domain/frequency-zh.txt`: the 50,000 most frequent Chinese words from wordfreq 3.1.1
  (CC BY-SA 4.0, blending SUBTLEX-CH and OpenSubtitles with other text), generated by
  `scripts/build-frequency.py`, 226 KB gzipped and precached. The storage worker fetches it once
  and gives it to the repository (`setFrequency`); without it new cards fall back to id order and
  a diagnostic is recorded.

Not changed: every tap still counts as a failure for the word's memory; the budget counts first
reviews today as before; seeded words keep their Anki schedule. Earned data is untouched, so the
thresholds and the list can change later by recomputation.

Rejected: library prevalence as the value signal (the reader: words common in the library are met
by watching); "5 encounters across 2 videos" (a proxy for library prevalence); pausing
low-frequency Anki words (the reader keeps everything already seen in Anki); a daily review cap
(the reader clears due cards as they come); SUBTLEX-CH directly (its data is "freely available for
research purposes", less clear for a public repository than wordfreq's CC BY-SA).
